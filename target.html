Target AU

Modernising the Checkout Architecture

Role
Product Designer
Checkout & Core Experience
Type
Large-scale E-commerce
Scope
Core Journey Redesign
Checkout & Navigation
Period
2021 — Present
01 Brief

The Challenge

Legacy Structure
The original checkout followed a rigid multi-page structure:

Step 1
Basket
Step 2
Delivery & Contact Details & Payment

The Core Problems

  • Delayed Stock Validation
    Stock wasn't accurately validated until customers entered a delivery address or selected a Click & Collect store. For delivery orders, out-of-stock items triggered an error, forcing customers to return to the basket and manually remove unavailable items (~5,000 cases/month). Click & Collect, however, automatically removed out-of-stock items.
    “Stock appears available but is out of stock at checkout. It’s so annoying when I only find out after I’ve gone through everything!”
    — Participant 12
  • Delivery Selection Friction
    78% of checkout customers were guests and exposed to OnePass promotions during checkout. Styled like delivery options, the promotions confused users and made it harder to identify actual delivery choices.
    “The OnePass option kept showing up and made the process confusing.”
    — Participant 13

The Objective
Re-architect to a single-page checkout that:

  • Validates real-time stock and delivery fees directly at the Basket stage.
  • Simplifies delivery options by removing promotional clutter.
02 Discover

The A/B Test Results

We ran a 2-week A/B test to validate the single-page checkout. While downstream steps improved, we found a clear issue right at the entry point.

+5.1%
Drop-off at cart landing compared to control
↓
Lower drop-off across all downstream checkout steps
$20K
AUD revenue loss due to the initial cart leak
03 Problem Space

Why the Basket Leaked

Target does not have a mini-cart. The basket page is the only place where users can review and edit their items before buying.

The Conflict
To solve the late-stage stock surprises, we moved the delivery and postcode selector to the top of the basket page.

  • This heavy layout shifted the hierarchy and blocked the product list view.
  • Users wanting to review their items were forced to make delivery decisions first, causing cognitive overload and the 4% spike in drop-offs.
04 Refinement Strategy

Balancing Fulfillment and Product Visibility

Rather than removing fulfillment selection, I reduced its visual footprint to better balance business needs with the customer's primary task. The goal was to validate stock early while restoring visibility of basket items.

Design Changes

  • Compact Fulfillment Selector
    Reduced the vertical space used by the fulfillment selector while keeping delivery information accessible.
  • Product Visibility
    Brought basket items back into the initial viewport so customers could review products without excessive scrolling.
  • Early Stock Validation
    Maintained early stock validation without introducing the heavy layout used in the original experiment.
05 Outcome
Handed Over

Delivered the refined basket experience with engineering-ready specifications.
The final solution balanced early stock validation with product visibility while creating a scalable foundation for future checkout improvements.

Key Takeaway

This project reminded me that good UX doesn't ask customers to think about business rules. It quietly supports them while helping people stay focused on what they came to do.
